How to Pivot Into Finance

A finance pivot requires more than an interest in markets or money. Employers need evidence that you understand the function, can work with financial information, and can communicate what the numbers mean.

  • 1Choose a finance function. Determine whether you are more interested in transactions, corporate decision-making, investments, risk, client relationships, or financial operations.
  • 2Translate your experience. Highlight transferable experience involving analysis, forecasting, budgeting, research, decision-making, client service, or risk management.
  • 3Build technical fluency. Identify the financial concepts, tools, and modeling skills expected in your target function.
  • 4Create evidence. Build a relevant model, analysis, investment thesis, company valuation, or financial recommendation.
  • 5Learn recruiting conventions. Finance functions have different recruiting timelines, interview formats, credentials, and entry points.
  • 6Target the right roles. Search for positions that match both your experience level and demonstrated technical ability.

You do not need to know every area of finance. You need to demonstrate that you understand your target function and can perform its core work.
